Event · single-occurrence time-bound experience

Short-duration, fixed-venue experience with a single date and time. Under 24 hours. Usually free or low-cost with a community meetup feel. The detail page leads with a prominent calendar date block because "when" is the single most important fact for an event — it determines whether the user can even consider attending. Distinct from scheduled experiences (which have multiple future date options and are multi-day).

Calendar date block is the hero
For an event, the single most important fact is when — it determines whether the user can even consider attending. The dark-ink header with month, Fraunces day number, tiny weekday label — it reads like a physical tear-off calendar. Placed first after the hero image. Contrast with scheduled experiences, where multiple dates appear as a horizontal rail lower in the page.
Key facts grid · 4 essentials
Starts, Duration, Difficulty, Spots left. All in a sunken card. These are the 4 facts that determine "is this for me?" before reading any description. Spots-left count creates soft urgency without being pushy — "3 of 15 left" is honest and scannable.
Meeting point is PUBLIC (unlike scheduled experiences)
For a scheduled experience we deliberately hid the exact pickup until 24hr before for privacy (the DD-021 scheduled_dates pattern). For events we show the exact meeting point immediately, because events are shorter, lower stakes, and usually free or low-cost. Users need to know if they can get there before RSVPing.
"Who's going" · social proof that converts
Overlapping avatar pile showing 4 attendees + "+8" count + "and 8 others going" copy. This is the single most conversion-critical element for community events. Users RSVP faster when they can see real people have already committed. Schema: read from event_occurrences.rsvp_count and the first 4 avatars from the bookings table.
WhatsApp share as first-class (DD-019)
The top nav has a dedicated WhatsApp button (green) alongside the generic share button. Indian users overwhelmingly share on WhatsApp — having a dedicated button saves a tap compared to opening the native share sheet and scrolling to find WhatsApp. Open Graph metadata (og:title, og:description, og:image, og:url) must be emitted on all public event pages so WhatsApp preview cards render cleanly.
Event vs scheduled experience · when to use which
Event: single date, under 24 hours, fixed venue, low-cost or free, community meetup feel. "Sunrise trek Saturday."
Scheduled experience: multiple future dates, multi-day, multi-location, higher price, commercial group trip. "Spiti 7-day trip running May and June."
Promoted from V2 to M0 (DD-025)
In SRS v1.1, events were deferred to V2 as "Events & Groups." DD-025 promotes them to M0 because the community meetup use case is too central to the platform's wedge — travel creators running weekend walks, photo meetups, food trails. Schema was already in the DB from M0; now the UI ships with them.
Free event variant (not drawn separately)
Free events follow the same template with two changes: (a) the sticky bottom bar shows "Free" in Fraunces instead of a rupee price, with CTA label "RSVP" instead of "Get my spot"; (b) the "Spots" fact shows "12 going / 50 max" since free events usually have looser capacity. All other sections (date block, meeting point, who's going, what to bring, cancellation) remain identical. Creators can publish free events without KYC (because no payment processing is involved) — this is a deliberate low-friction entry point for new community organizers.
