The map IS the hero · fundamental shift
Unlike every other detail page where the hero is a photo, the self-paced itinerary hero is the MAP. This immediately communicates "this is a spot-based guide, not a narrative." Users who have used Roamy, Google Maps lists, or any trip planner recognize the pattern in under a second.
Numbered pins · coral for overnight stop · DD-024
Stops 1-3 are ink pins with white numbers. The LAST stop of each day (stop 4, the Jibhi homestay where the user sleeps) is a larger coral pin. This is one of the 5 coral contexts — the overnight stop is psychologically the most important spot of each day (sleep, logistics, anchor point). Other days' spots appear on the map as muted gray dots.
Map / List toggle
Bottom-right of the map. "Map" is the default view. Tapping "List" collapses the map and expands the scrollable stop list to use the full viewport. Classic Roamy pattern. Engineers: handle the state with a simple local toggle — no need for a router change.
Trip stats bar · 4 facts at a glance
Days, Spots, km, Budget. All in Fraunces with tabular numbers. The sunken card sits just below the creator chip. These are the facts a user needs to decide "is this trip for me?" before reading any details.
Spot card anatomy
Each spot: numbered badge (top-left overlay on thumbnail), category overline, name, duration, "Open in Maps" deep-link. Below, the creator note in warm sunken background with ink left-border and italic Fraunces. The note is the single most valuable piece of content on the page — it's why someone pays ₹499. The design prioritizes the note visually.
Freemium · Day 1 partial preview
Stops 1-2 of Day 1 are fully visible with complete notes. Stops 3-4 and Days 2-7 are replaced by a dashed-border preview card showing "22 more spots across 6 days" with named highlights (Key Monastery, Dhankar Lake, Chandratal) to tease the value. Day 2-7 chips show lock icons. The free preview is generous enough to establish value, constrained enough to drive purchase.
Post-purchase state (not shown)
After buying, the sticky bar changes to "Continue · 3 of 24 spots visited" with a progress pill. All days unlock. Each spot gets a "Mark as visited" button. Visited spots turn into checkmarks on the map. The itinerary becomes a personal checklist the user follows on their actual trip. This reuses the same layout — only the bottom bar and spot states change.
Publishing wizard implication
This screen's structure dictates what the publishing wizard needs: a spot editor (not a text editor). Creators search places via Google Places autocomplete, drop them into day buckets, write a 1-2 sentence creator note, mark one spot per day as overnight. Full wizard design is Screen 07 (pending).
